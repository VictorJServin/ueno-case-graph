# Ueno Bank S.A. (Causa 89/2026): defence-side assessment of the allegations

Prepared 20 November 2026 by an AI legal analyst. This is analysis for an informed reader, not legal advice, and its author is not a licensed lawyer. Everything about wrongdoing in the case is allegation. Every person named is presumed innocent. The memo argues the best good-faith defence positions available on the alleged facts and the law. It invents no facts, and its last section says which of its own arguments are weak.

Sources: the 24-page prosecution record (`src:acta-pNN`), the record data (`rec:*`), press data (`press:*`), the two press reports, and `analysis/legal_framework.md` ("the framework"). The framework read statutes through a tool that returned paraphrase and short fragments, so exact wording must be checked before any filing.

## 1. What the record does and does not say

The record is the acta of Julio Rey's investigative statement. Pages 1-19 summarise the bank supervisor's findings; pages 19-24 are Rey's own account. Three features matter for every offence.

- **It attributes everything to a list of eight people as a block.** Seven directors and the síndico "autorizaron y permitieron" the operations "entre los años 2025 y 2026" (`src:acta-p01`), as co-authors under CP Art. 29 inc. 2 (`src:acta-p19`). No vote, minute, committee, signature or date is tied to any individual.
- **It describes risk, not a quantified loss.** The operations "habrían comprometido en forma negativa activos del banco" (`src:acta-p01`) and compromised "la posibilidad de pago" (`src:acta-p02`). No loss figure appears anywhere in the record.
- **It gives four offences one line of classification and no subsumption** (`src:acta-p19`). It does not say which facts fit which offence, who was deceived, or which balance sheet was wrong.

## 2. Art. 192, lesión de confianza (breach of trust)

**Elements.** (1) A duty, assumed by law, administrative resolution or contract, to protect a relevant patrimonial interest of another; (2) causing, or not preventing, a patrimonial loss; (3) within the sphere of protection entrusted; (4) intent. There is no negligent variant and no punishable attempt. The most contestable elements are loss, intent, and each individual's contribution.

**Defence on the law**

- *No loss, no offence.* Art. 192 is a five-year delito with no attempt clause, and CP Art. 27 punishes attempted delitos only where the law says so. A loan that merely endangers recovery is not yet a loss. The prosecution's own choice of "tentativa" for the fraud count suggests it did not regard the damage as complete when it wrote the record. Paraguayan doctrine cited in the framework (Royg Acha) requires a relevant loss and excludes attempt and negligence. The framework found no Paraguayan ruling stating when related-party lending becomes a "perjuicio patrimonial", so the point is open.
- *The mode charged does not fit everyone.* The record charges inc. 1 "modalidad causara", the active form (`src:acta-p19`). A síndico does not grant credit, and a director who did not vote on an operation did not "cause" it. For them only "no evitara" could fit, and that is not what was charged.
- *Prudential breach is not a crime.* Ley 861/96 Art. 38 and Ley 489/95 Arts. 89-95 already sanction directors who approve operations in breach of banking rules, with fines and disqualification. The defence says the supervisor's findings (weak credit analysis, long terms, no collateral) are the vocabulary of that regime.
- *The "legal cap" has no retrieved source.* The statutory 20% related-party cap (Ley 861/96 Art. 59) and the single-borrower caps (Art. 64) were repealed in 2016. Current limits are whatever the Central Bank fixed under Art. 58, and the framework could not retrieve that resolution. The record cites no article, resolution number or percentage for the limit it says was exceeded. Until the rule is produced, "above the legal cap" (as in `src:abc-2026-10-10-tres-empresas-tope-legal`) is an assertion.

**Defence on the facts**

- *Regulator knowledge and approval.* Rey states that a 20-year feasibility plan prepared in May-June 2024 "fue aprobado por el BCP", that it contemplated permanent transactions among related companies, and that the supervisor had "acceso libre, directo y permanente" to the bank's movements (`src:acta-p20`, `rec:event:0044`). Press reports of merger facilities authorised on 27 May 2024 are consistent with a plan existing. A director who acts inside a model the regulator approved and watched has a real argument against intent.
- *Unanswered de-linking request.* The bank asked in October 2025 to de-list MOWI, Hendy and Grupo M; the record says the supervisor gave no formal reply (`src:acta-p02`, `rec:event:0055`). The supervisor confirmed related status only on 5 June 2026 (`press:event:0005`). One headline says the regulator let the bank operate for ten months (`src:ultimahora-2026-11-10-cronologia`). The defence reads that as tolerance that an individual director could rely on.
- *Business judgment and group synergy.* Rey describes a digital bank that "no podía funcionar" without ITTI, WEPA and REDIPRO (`src:acta-p20`). Money moving from borrowers to those firms can be payment for technology and network services the bank needed.
- *Cancelled loans.* The record concedes that "varias de las operaciones fueron canceladas" (`src:acta-p10`; e.g. `rec:loan:0003`, `rec:loan:0005`, `rec:loan:0007`, the early FRANEDE loans and most AIME credits). A repaid loan caused no loss.
- *The Vinanzas sale brought cash in.* Nine loans classed in the best category (1a) were assigned without recourse for USD 74,418,418.12 paid in cash (`rec:flow:0060`, `rec:flow:0061`).
- *Group C.* The prosecution itself says there are no signs of linkage for six further borrowers (`src:acta-p16`). Lending to them is ordinary credit risk.
- *Who decided.* Rey calls Miguel Vázquez "la cabeza principal y el máximo decisor" (`src:acta-p21`, `rec:role:0027`). Other directors will say decisions were taken above them and that they relied on the risk area, the legal area and the external auditor. Ley 489/95 Art. 91 exempts those who did not know and had no reasonable indications.
- *Time on the board.* Rey says he left the board in July 2026 and took no part in later credits such as Galadriel (`src:acta-p23`, `rec:event:0066`; the Galadriel loan is dated 31 July 2026 in `press:event:0034`). The alternate síndico ceased on 27 March 2026 (`rec:event:0062`). The record's undifferentiated "2025 y 2026" ignores this.

## 3. Art. 179, conducta indebida en situaciones de crisis, and its Art. 178 condition

**Elements.** (1) Insolvency or illiquidity that is imminent or has occurred; (2) in that situation, drawing up or altering balance sheets, contrary to law, so that the real position is hard to know (núm. 6 a); (3) intent; (4) the objective condition of Art. 178 inc. 2: the business has ceased payments or been declared bankrupt, and a link with the conduct cannot be excluded.

**Defence on the law.** This is the defence's best statutory point. The Central Bank opened the resolution under Ley 2334/03 Art. 13 **b)**, a solvency ratio below half the legal minimum (`src:acta-p01`, `rec:event:0067`). Art. 13 **a)** is the separate ground for a bank that has suspended payment of its obligations, and it was not invoked. No bankruptcy has been declared; under Ley 861/96 Art. 152 only the Central Bank can ask for one, after liquidation. Criminal law is read strictly, so a solvency-ratio resolution is neither "cesación de pagos" nor "quiebra". The framework itself records that the text does not answer whether a resolution satisfies the condition. If it does not, Art. 179 is not punishable at all, whatever the facts.

**Defence on the facts.** The record identifies no balance sheet: no date, no line item, no signatory, no accounting rule breached. Its only sentence on the point is that the Vinanzas sale "impediría conocer el estado real" of the bank's position (`src:acta-p19`). The sale was documented and, on the reported BCP regulation, notifiable within ten business days, so the supervisor saw it. Press accounts say the external auditor signed the 2025 accounts without qualification; a director who is not an accountant can point to that. The reported solvency ratio was 13.85% against a 12% minimum until supervisory adjustments, which the defence will use to contest that a "crisis" existed, or was known, when the accounts were drawn up.

## 4. Art. 187, estafa, charged as attempt

**Elements.** (1) A false statement about a fact; (2) which causes an error in another person; (3) who therefore disposes of assets; (4) patrimonial loss; (5) intent to obtain an undue benefit.

**Defence.** The record never says who was deceived, by what statement, or what that person handed over. Each candidate fails on the record as written:

- *The bank* cannot be deceived by its own board; that is the breach-of-trust theory, not fraud.
- *The supervisor* makes no disposition of assets, and on Rey's account was not in error (`src:acta-p20`). It confirmed the links itself on 5 June 2026 (`press:event:0005`) and analysed the Vinanzas sale within weeks (`press:event:0008`, `press:event:0010`).
- *Depositors* are not mentioned in connection with any specific false statement.

Charging an attempt means the prosecution accepted that error, disposition or loss did not occur. The appeals court's reported recasting to consummated aggravated fraud added no new fact. Without a named victim and statement the count is not answerable, which is itself a defence objection to the sufficiency of the charge (CPP Arts. 86 and 302 require the act attributed to be described).

## 5. Art. 181, books of commerce

**Elements.** Drawing up balance sheets, contrary to law, in a way that makes the real position hard to know (inc. 1 núm. 3 a), with intent (the negligent form carries at most one year).

**Defence.** (1) The same single sentence in the record supports both Art. 179 núm. 6 a) and Art. 181 núm. 3 a), in near-identical words. Punishing both is double counting; one displaces the other. (2) The framework records an unresolved conflict on Art. 181 inc. 3: one reading cross-refers to Art. 178 inc. 2. If that reading is right, this count also falls for lack of cessation of payments or bankruptcy. (3) The duty to draw up accounts sits with the merchant and its accountant (Ley 1034/83 Art. 77). The record says nothing about who prepared or approved which statement. (4) Classifying a loan as non-related while a de-linking request is pending, or treating a documented cash sale as a sale, is an accounting judgment later disputed by the supervisor, not a falsified book.

## 6. Art. 196, lavado de activos (reportedly added on appeal)

The record contains no laundering charge and describes no laundering facts (`src:acta-p19`). Press reports say the appeals court added it on 10 November.

- *Procedure.* CPP Arts. 456-457 confine an appeal court to the points challenged and forbid worsening the position of an accused who alone appealed. One defence has reportedly taken this to the Supreme Court, saying nobody appealed the classification. On the text, this is strong. Its practical reach is limited, because the prosecution can still include the offence in its accusation.
- *Predicate.* Laundering needs an object that stems from an unlawful act. Arts. 179 and 181 are not predicates. If breach of trust and fraud fail, so does laundering.
- *Same conduct twice.* Forwarding loan money to group companies is the very conduct said to constitute the breach of trust. Calling the next transfer "laundering" punishes one course of events twice.
- *No concealment.* The transfers ran through accounts at the bank itself and the interbank system, and the supervisor traced them from the bank's own records (`rec:flow:0001` to `rec:flow:0040`).
- *Recipients.* An officer of a recipient company needs knowledge of the unlawful origin. Receiving a transfer from a sister company for invoices or overdrafts does not show it. At most the gross-negligence form (two years or fine) is in play.

## 7. Art. 239, asociación criminal (reportedly added on appeal)

The offence requires an association organised to commit punishable acts. The defence says a licensed bank and a corporate group with real businesses, audited accounts and millions of customers are organised for lawful commerce. A board is a statutory organ, not a criminal structure. Shared corporate roles (`src:acta-p21`) show a group, not a conspiracy. Co-authorship under Art. 29 inc. 2 already covers acting together; Art. 239 would otherwise attach to every board decision later found unlawful. The same procedural objection applies as for Art. 196. The framework checked only the 1997 text of Art. 239, so any later amendment is unverified. Art. 239 inc. 3 lets the court waive the penalty for secondary contributions, which matters for peripheral accused.

## 8. Evidence and data weaknesses

- **A co-accused's statement.** Rey's statement is an unsworn act of defence (CPP Arts. 84-88). Press reports say his lawyer is seeking a cooperation agreement and that a rating report listed him as a group director at end-2025. Other accused will say he has a motive to shift blame and that his word needs independent corroboration. The framework contains no statutory rule on the weight of a co-accused's statement, so this is an argument about weight, not exclusion.
- **Hearsay inside it.** The cash delivery of about USD 17 million and Gs. 17,000 million is what Lucca de Barba told Rey; Rey did not see it and is unsure who drove (`rec:flow:0071`, `rec:flow:0072`, `rec:event:0047`). He dates it to about May 2025 inside a passage about enquiries from October 2025 (`rec:issue:0009`). The USD 900 million figure is approximate and not broken down (`rec:flow:0075`). Six of his claims have no press counterpart.
- **Diagrams.** Much of the linkage evidence is small print in diagrams. Eighteen role rows and twelve flow rows are low confidence, including the roles attributed to the síndico in TUIU and AIME (`rec:role:0090`, `rec:role:0091`), the ITTI "home-banking operators" (`rec:role:0115` to `rec:role:0120`), and most of the June 2026 bond circuit (`rec:flow:0048` to `rec:flow:0059`). Page 12 was scanned upside down.
- **Internal errors.** The caption names a different second surname for the lead suspect (`rec:issue:0001`). "3 groups" are announced and four follow (`rec:issue:0002`). The supervisor is called "de seguros" (`rec:issue:0003`). The sale price and the sum of the loans differ by one unit (`rec:issue:0004`). The Grupo M officer table repeats rows from MOWI and Hendy (`rec:issue:0005`), which goes directly to who held office where. FRANEDE's capital is printed at an implausible figure (`rec:issue:0006`) and three balances exceed principal (`rec:issue:0007`). The AIME sentence is garbled (`rec:issue:0008`). Totals use different exchange rates (`rec:issue:0010`). The Vinanzas balance is 93,628 in the record's diagram and 93,828 in the press (`rec:event:0064`, `press:fact:0036`).
- **Date.** The record is dated 4 November 2026; the press places Rey's statement on 17 or 18 November (`rec:issue:0011`). The defence of other accused will ask which act this document records and whether CPP formalities were met for it.

## 9. Positions by role

- **Board member.** Co-authorship needs a personal contribution giving shared control. The defence asks for the minutes: who voted, on what, with what information. Reliance on the risk and legal areas, the approved plan, the unqualified audit and the absence of supervisory objection goes to intent. Periods off the board are excluded.
- **Statutory auditor (síndico).** A síndico oversees and reports; he does not grant credit, so "causara" does not fit. Art. 192 has no negligent form, so failure to detect is not enough. The framework did not retrieve the Civil Code articles on síndico duties, and the diagram rows tying the síndico to borrower companies are low confidence.
- **Nominal shareholder or officer of a borrower.** Art. 192 requires a duty to protect the bank's patrimony. A borrower has none. Such a person can be at most an accomplice or instigator, with mandatory mitigation (CP Arts. 31-32), and only with knowledge of the directors' breach. Taking shares or an office days before a loan is suspicious but is not itself a contribution to the bank's decision. Several were at liberty as of 16 November, per the press.
- **Officer of a recipient company.** Receiving funds from a group borrower is neutral without proof of knowledge. No officer of Vinanzas or the five June 2026 borrowers appears among the 27 by that affiliation, per the press.
- **Rey.** His position combines partial contest and cooperation: he was a group operations director, not the bank's (`src:acta-p21`); he left in July 2026; he raised concerns after the October 2025 email; his lawyer says he will answer "por lo que hizo, pero no por lo que no hizo". Paraguay has no general cooperating-defendant statute. The routes that exist are: mitigation or waiver under CP Art. 196 inc. 9 and Art. 239 inc. 4 (available only because those offences were added); the opportunity criterion (CPP Art. 19), which needs reparation or willingness to repair; abbreviated procedure (CPP Arts. 420-421), whose five-year threshold wording is unresolved in the framework although it was used in the Banco Alemán case; conditional suspension (its article was not read); and alternatives to pre-trial prison (CPP Art. 245). None is guaranteed, and an admission in a rejected abbreviated procedure cannot be used as a confession.

## 10. Former public officials (Case 92/2026), as reported only

Press reports say the former president declares himself innocent and declined to testify. His lawyer reportedly argued that the accusation rests on "indicios"; that no act or file signed by him favouring the bank is identified; that the alleged scheme was built after he left the company; that the Central Bank itself detected the irregularities and filed the complaint; that he never held shares in the bank itself; and that a president does not tell agencies where to bank. The former Central Bank president declined to testify; the regulator's reported position is that he recused himself from Ueno decisions. No statements from the other former central bankers were found. This memo takes no position on those claims.

## 11. Which defence arguments look weakest, and why

- **"The loans were repaid."** The record says the repayments came from other group companies, funded by overdrafts and 72-hour cheques (`src:acta-p05`, `src:acta-p11`, `src:acta-p14`). If that is proved, cancellation shows circular funding, not recovery.
- **"The Vinanzas sale brought in cash."** The record traces the price to USD 81.841 million of new unsecured loans the bank granted days earlier, and says the buyer held under USD 100,000 the day before (`rec:event:0064`, `rec:event:0065`). The bank would have swapped one exposure for another.
- **"The regulator approved it."** The plan dates from 2024. Nothing in the record shows it covered sole-signature loans to companies whose shares changed hands days earlier (`rec:event:0053`, `rec:event:0054`). Rey himself says the flexibilities ended between October 2025 and March 2026 (`src:acta-p22`), yet lending continued to June 2026. Press data say the regulator refused the Mowi de-linking (`press:event:0004`) and that supporting documents were sent only in May 2026 (`press:event:0046`). The former regulators are themselves charged, so their tolerance may be treated as part of the problem.
- **"Only risk, no loss."** A bank resolved with a reported provisions deficit of about Gs. 1.62 trillion gives the prosecution a route to quantify loss, and the BNT precedent shows ten-year sentences for bank directors under Art. 192.
- **"Rey was off the board."** He left in July 2026. The six borrowers' loans and the 15 June 2026 sale all fall before that.
- **Ne bis in idem.** The administrative proceeding opened on 24 September 2026 (`press:event:0011`), but Ley 861/96 Art. 38 and Ley 489/95 expressly leave criminal action open.
- **"Shares were sold, so the borrowers were unrelated."** Share transfers one to four days around the first loans, shared officers and identical account operators are the facts Ley 861/96 Art. 47 was written for.
