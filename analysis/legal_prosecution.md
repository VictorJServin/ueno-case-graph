# Ueno Bank S.A. (Causa 89/2026): the prosecution-side assessment

**What this is.** An analysis by an AI legal analyst, not a licensed lawyer. It sets out the strongest good-faith case a prosecutor could build from the facts *alleged* in the 24-page record and the project data. It is not legal advice and not a finding of guilt. Every person named is presumed innocent. "The record alleges" means the Ministerio Público's summary of the bank supervisor's findings (pages 1-19); "Rey says" means the suspect's own statement (pages 19-24). The two voices are kept apart.

**Limits stated up front.**

- The legal texts come from `legal_framework.md`, which read the statutes through a tool that returned paraphrase plus short fragments. Exact wording must be checked before any filing.
- No BCP resolution was retrieved as primary text. That covers the related-party limits, the portfolio-sale rule (Res. 15/24) and the provisioning rules. Wherever the case depends on "the rule prohibits X", the rule itself is unverified here.
- The record is a statement record, not the indictment. It attributes the conduct to the board collectively ("autorizaron y permitieron", src:acta-p01) and does not say who voted for what.
- The offences added on appeal (laundering, criminal association, consummated aggravated fraud) are known only from press reports of a 10 Nov 2026 ruling. The ruling was not read.
- The record is dated 4 Nov 2026; the press places Rey's statement on 18 Nov 2026 (rec:issue:0011).

---

## 1. The alleged scheme in one paragraph

Between 2025 and 2026 the bank lent large sums on signature alone, over 7 to 10 years, to companies with little income: MOWI, Grupo Hendy and Grupo M (declared related, then "de-listed" on paper), TUIU, FRANEDE and AIME (never declared related), plus six others showing the same pattern (src:acta-p02, p10, p16). The record alleges the money went almost immediately to group companies (ITTI, REDISA, REDIPRO, Vinanzas, Grupo Vázquez) and that repayments came from those same companies, not from the borrowers (rec:flow:0001 to 0040). When the supervisor kept treating the first three as related, the bank sold nine of their loans to Vinanzas on 15 June 2026 for USD 74,418,418.12 "in cash" (rec:flow:0060). The record alleges the cash traced back to five new bank loans totalling USD 81,841,000 made on 11 and 15 June (rec:loan:0036 to 0040), and that Vinanzas had no genuine funds of its own the day before (rec:event:0064). On 2 Nov 2026 the BCP ordered resolution for solvency below half the legal minimum (rec:event:0067).

## 2. Art. 192, lesión de confianza (breach of trust), including the aggravated frame

**Penalty.** Up to five years or a fine; "especially grave" cases up to ten years (inc. 2). The record cites inc. 1, "modalidad causara" (src:acta-p19). It does not cite inc. 2.

**Elements and the alleged facts.**

1. *A duty, based on law, administrative resolution or contract, to protect another's relevant patrimonial interest.* The record names seven board members and the síndico and says the bank's assets were mainly third-party funds they were bound to safeguard (src:acta-p01; rec:role:0001 to 0016). Ley 861/96 Art. 38 makes directors "especially responsible" for approving operations in breach of financial rules. This is the best-supported element.
2. *Causing (or not preventing) a patrimonial loss within the protected sphere.* The conduct side is well documented: 35 itemised operations with dates, numbers, terms and "a sola firma" (rec:loan:0001 to 0035); Hendy with negative net equity and average monthly income of Gs. 73 million (src:acta-p05); Grupo M with long-term debt 23.5 times equity and 11.85 times income (src:acta-p08); MOWI found to have no ordinary income capacity (src:acta-p04); FRANEDE reported as mortgage-backed on the strength of an uninstrumented commitment letter (src:acta-p14). The fund tracing shows who benefited (rec:flow:0001 to 0040) and that repayment depended on the group (rec:flow:0008 to 0013, 0020, 0024). Stated exposures: MOWI Gs. 254,157 million, Hendy Gs. 252,521 million, Grupo M Gs. 237,106 million, FRANEDE about Gs. 314,001 million, AIME Gs. 65,546 million of risk balance (src:acta-p03, p04, p07, p12, p15).
3. *Intent (at least dolo eventual; no negligent form, no attempt).* Circumstantial indicators: share transfers to former employees and relatives days before or after the first loans (rec:event:0050, 0052, 0053, 0054, 0060); the unsupported de-listing request of October 2025 with lending continuing (rec:event:0055); the same pattern across twelve borrowers; and, in the diagrams, the same twelve home-banking operators, described as ITTI staff, on the borrowers' accounts (rec:role:0115 to 0120, low confidence).

**Aggravated frame (inc. 2).** A prosecutor would argue scale (hundreds of millions of dollars), duration, the use of depositors' funds, and the bank's collapse. The framework cites the BNT case (ten-year terms for bank directors) only through a court press note, so it is a pointer, not authority read in full.

**Evidence still needed.**

- Board and credit-committee minutes, votes, dissents and approval limits for each operation. Without them co-authorship (Art. 29 inc. 2) rests on title alone.
- An expert accounting opinion on *loss*: recoverable value at the date of each loan, provisions required, and how much of the Gs. 1.62 trillion ("1,62 billones") provisioning deficit reported in the press (press:fact:0105) belongs to these operations.
- Credit files, risk reports and the "Gestión de Riesgo de Crédito" reports the record mentions (src:acta-p14).
- Bank statements and SIPAP records behind the tracing, which the record summarises but does not attach.

**Where the record is thin.**

- *Loss is asserted, not quantified.* Totals track outstanding balances at inconsistent exchange rates (rec:issue:0010); several loans were cancelled (src:acta-p10); nine were sold at face value. A defence will say an unpaid loan is a risk, not yet a loss. The framework found no Paraguayan decision stating when related-party lending becomes a "perjuicio patrimonial".
- *Individual attribution.* Nothing in the record ties a named director to a named approval.
- *Group C* (six companies) is one paragraph with no figures (src:acta-p16).
- *Wording.* Whether the 2008 text says "obligación" or "responsabilidad" de proteger is unresolved in the framework.

**By role.**

- *Director who approved:* the natural author; exposure depends on proof of the vote and of knowledge of the borrower's condition.
- *Director who did not vote, or who left:* the "no evitara" form could apply, but the record charges "causara". Rey says he left in July 2026 and took no part in some loans (src:acta-p23).
- *Síndico:* an oversight role, so the natural theory is omission. The source of the duty (Código Civil síndico articles) was not retrieved, and "causara" fits poorly. The diagram also shows him as síndico of two borrowers (rec:role:0090, 0091, low confidence), which a prosecutor would want to confirm.
- *Group executive with a dual role:* Rey describes each director as also holding a group-wide post (rec:role:0018 to 0025). That supports knowledge of where the money went and a conflict of interest.
- *Nominal shareholder or officer of a borrower:* owes no duty to the bank, so cannot be author of Art. 192. Liability would be as instigator or accomplice (Arts. 30-32), with the mitigated frame, and needs proof they knew the board was breaching its duty.

## 3. Art. 179, conducta indebida en situaciones de crisis, and its Art. 178 condition

**Penalty.** Up to five years or a fine (negligent variants up to two years). The record relies on inc. 1 núm. 6 a): drawing up or altering balance sheets, contrary to law, so that the real position is hard to know.

**Elements and the alleged facts.**

1. *Insolvency or illiquidity, imminent or occurred.* Resolution for solvency below 50% of the minimum (rec:event:0067). Rey says the "Delirio Colectivo" attachment of 13 Oct 2025 showed "the real liquidity channels" and that he worried about sustainability from October 2025 to March 2026 (rec:event:0056; src:acta-p22). The tracing shows repayments funded by overdrafts and 72-hour cheques (rec:flow:0026 to 0028, 0031, 0032). A prosecutor would use these to date the crisis back to late 2025.
2. *Balance sheets drawn up or altered, contrary to law, so as to obscure the position.* The record's theory: the portfolio "sale" left the bank's books showing cash received and risky related loans gone, while the bank had itself funded the purchase (src:acta-p17 to p19; rec:flow:0041 to 0061). The record says this "would prevent knowing the real state of its patrimonial situation" (src:acta-p19). Supporting facts: the nine sold loans were all carried in the best client category, 1a (rec:flow:0062 to 0070), and FRANEDE loans were reported as mortgage-backed (src:acta-p14).
3. *Objective condition of punishability (Art. 178 inc. 2):* the author or the business has ceased payments or been declared bankrupt, and a link with the conduct cannot be excluded.

**The Art. 178 condition is the weak point.** The resolution rested on Ley 2334/03 Art. 13 b) (solvency), not Art. 13 a) (suspension of payments) (src:acta-p01). No bankruptcy exists, and under Ley 861/96 Art. 152 only the BCP may seek one, after liquidation. Whether a resolution process counts as "cesación de pagos" is a question the statute does not answer. A prosecutor would need evidence of actual unpaid obligations (failed clearing, unpaid deposits, the suspension of liabilities under Ley 2334 Art. 14) and a legal argument that this satisfies the condition. Press reports say Art. 178 itself was added for one defendant; the same condition applies to it. The Art. 180 aggravation refers to Art. 178, not Art. 179.

**Evidence still needed.** The specific balance sheets and regulatory returns (June 2026 and later), who prepared, signed and approved them, the accounting entries for the sale, the external auditor's working papers, and the BCP accounting rule said to be breached.

**Thin spots.** The record identifies no balance sheet, date or signatory. It speaks of an effect ("impediría conocer") rather than a document.

**By role.** Authorship fits those who draw up or approve the accounts: the board that approves them, the general manager and the accountant (jointly responsible under Ley 1034/83 Art. 77), none of the last two being named in the record. The síndico's review of the accounts makes this count closer to his role than Art. 192. Borrower-company officers are remote from this offence.

## 4. Art. 187, estafa (fraud), attempt and the reported aggravated form

**Penalty.** Up to five years or a fine; attempt punishable (inc. 2); especially grave cases up to eight years (inc. 3). The record charges attempt (src:acta-p19). The press reports the appeals court recast it as consummated and aggravated.

**Elements.** A false statement about a fact; an error caused in another person; a disposition of assets by that person; a patrimonial loss; intent to obtain an undue benefit.

**The record never says who was deceived.** That is the central gap. The candidates a prosecutor could develop:

| Deceived party | False statement | Disposition and loss | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| The supervisor (SIB/BCP) | The sale "al contado" (src:acta-p17); the de-listing request without support (rec:event:0055); mortgages reported that did not exist (src:acta-p14) | None obvious: a supervisor that is misled does not hand over assets. Would need a BCP or deposit-fund outlay induced by the falsehood | False statement supported; disposition missing |
| Depositors and investors | Financial statements and solvency figures that concealed related exposure | Deposits or CDA purchases; loss on resolution | Plausible for a consummated theory, but the record has no depositor evidence |
| The capital side (regulator, co-shareholder OTP) | Rey: CDAs bought in 2025 with cash "justified" by consecutive-numbered receipts from a "dead portfolio", which in 2026 "se pretendía" use to capitalise the bank (rec:flow:0071 to 0073; src:acta-p22) | Not completed, which fits *attempt* | A lead from Rey only; hearsay; needs documents |
| The bank itself (deceived by borrowers) | Declared loan purpose different from real destination (src:acta-p02, p14) | The loans | Conflicts with Art. 192: if the board knew, the bank was not in error |

**Evidence still needed.** The representations actually made (notes to the SIB, the sale contract and its notification, regulatory returns, prospectuses, capital filings); who relied on them and what they did as a result; for the consummated theory, identified victims and losses.

**Assessment.** The false-statement element is partly supported by the record. Error, disposition and loss are thin. The step from attempt to consummated aggravated fraud is legally uncertain: it came from the appeals court, reportedly of its own motion, and defences invoke CPP Arts. 456-457 (appeal limited to the points raised; no worsening on the accused's own appeal).

**By role.** Whoever made or signed the representation is the natural author. Borrower-company officers who signed loan applications stating a false purpose could be authors only on the "bank deceived" theory, which the prosecution's own case undermines; otherwise they are possible accomplices.

## 5. Art. 181, violación del deber de llevar libros de comercio

**Penalty.** Up to five years or a fine; negligent form up to one year. The record relies on inc. 1 núm. 3 a).

**Elements.** A legal duty to keep books (Ley 1034/83 Arts. 74-85; SIB accounting rules); drawing up balance sheets, contrary to law, in a way that hinders knowledge of the real patrimonial position; intent (or negligence for the lesser form).

**Alleged facts.** The same as Art. 179 element 2: the self-funded portfolio sale booked as a cash sale (rec:flow:0041 to 0061; src:acta-p19), the 1a classification (rec:flow:0062 to 0070), the reported mortgages (src:acta-p14), and related parties carried as unrelated (rec:event:0055; src:acta-p10). Rey adds that group expenses were turned into loans "with the aim of deferring those expenses" (src:acta-p22; rec:flow:0075), which, if proved, means the loan book itself misdescribed expenses as assets.

**Why a prosecutor wants this count.** It is the fallback if the crisis chapeau of Art. 179 fails. But the framework flags an unresolved conflict: Art. 181 inc. 3 may cross-refer to Art. 178 inc. 2 (the same cessation-of-payments condition) or to Art. 178 inc. 1 núm. 3. If the former, the fallback has the same weakness.

**Evidence needed and by role.** As for Art. 179. The negligent form matters for the síndico and for directors without accounting functions.

## 6. Offences reportedly added on appeal

### Art. 196, lavado de activos

**Penalty.** Up to five years or a fine; up to ten when acting commercially or as a member of a gang (inc. 4).

**Elements.** An object stemming from a listed predicate (Arts. 187 and 192 are listed; Arts. 179 and 181 are not); converting or concealing it or disguising its origin; knowledge. It is an autonomous offence (inc. 10).

**Alleged facts a prosecutor would use.** The layering pattern: disbursement, immediate transfer to group companies, onward SIPAP transfers to accounts at other banks (rec:flow:0004 to 0007), 72-hour cheques (rec:flow:0026 to 0028), and in June 2026 the routing of new loans through bond purchases and "contracts" before the money reached Vinanzas (rec:flow:0046 to 0059, mostly low-confidence diagram readings). From Rey: about USD 17 million and Gs. 17,000 million in cash brought to the bank at night and booked as collections of unpaid loans (rec:flow:0071, 0072; rec:event:0047).

**Weaknesses.** It is legally uncertain whether loan proceeds obtained through a breach of trust are an "object stemming from" that offence when the movement of the money *is* the breach; the framework does not address self-laundering. For the cash, no predicate is identified: its origin is unknown. Evidence needed: SEPRELAD reports, a full fund-flow expert report, the receipts and the people named on them, treasury count sheets.

**By role.** Officers of recipient and pass-through companies are most exposed here, more than under Art. 192, because inc. 2 covers receiving and passing on with knowledge, and inc. 5 covers gross negligence as to origin (up to two years).

### Art. 239, asociación criminal

**Penalty.** Up to five years.

**Elements.** An association, hierarchical or otherwise organised, set up to commit punishable acts; membership, support or services.

**Alleged facts.** Rey describes a single decision-maker and directors with dual bank and group roles (rec:role:0018 to 0027); the record shows a repeated method across borrowers, with shares placed in the names of former employees and relatives (rec:role:0033, 0049, 0074, 0093, 0101) and common operators.

**Weaknesses.** A corporate group is a lawful organisation. The prosecution must prove it was organised *to commit offences*, not that offences were committed inside it. The record, written before the appeal ruling, contains no allegation in those terms. The framework checked only the 1997 text of Art. 239.

## 7. Liability by role: summary

| Role | Most natural offences | What must be proved |
|---|---|---|
| Board member who approved | 192 (author), 179/181, 187 | The vote; knowledge of borrower weakness and fund destination |
| Board member with a group-wide post | Same, with stronger knowledge inference | The dual role (so far from Rey only) and what information it gave |
| Síndico | 181/179 (incl. negligent forms); 192 by omission | Source and scope of the duty; what he reviewed |
| Nominal shareholder or officer of a borrower | Complicity in 192; 196 inc. 2 or 5 | Knowledge; who actually operated the accounts |
| Officer of a recipient company | 196; complicity in 192 | Knowledge of origin; role in onward transfers |

The record lists borrower-company officers as persons who "habrían participado" (src:acta-p04, p06, p09, p12, p14, p16). That is an allegation of involvement, not of any specific act.

## 8. Rey's statement: value to the prosecution

**Formal validity.** The record shows two defence lawyers present and the Art. 86 warnings given (src:acta-p01, p19), which is what CPP Arts. 84-86 and 96 require for the statement to be usable.

**Admissions useful to the prosecution.**

- He sat on the bank's board from July 2024 to July 2026 (rec:role:0026), which covers every itemised loan and the 15 June 2026 portfolio sale.
- He says the model involved "permanent and constant" transactions with related companies (src:acta-p20) and that expenses were turned into related and non-related loans to defer them, naming Hendy, Mowi, Grupo M, TUIU, Franede and others, for about USD 900 million (rec:flow:0075). That supports the allegation that declared loan purposes were not the real ones.
- He received the "Delirio Colectivo" email on 13 Oct 2025 and was concerned from then (rec:event:0056). Lending continued until May 2026 while he remained a director. A prosecutor would use this for knowledge and for "no evitara".
- He places the top decision-making with one person and describes dual roles for each director (rec:role:0018 to 0027).

**What cuts the other way and must be met.** He says the feasibility plan with these "facilities" was approved by the BCP in 2024 and that the regulator had permanent access (rec:event:0044; src:acta-p20); that he was not the bank's operations director (src:acta-p21); and that he had left before some loans (src:acta-p23).

**New leads.** The cash delivery and the people named around it; three consecutive-numbered receipts and CDA schedules showing endorsements (handed over, src:acta-p24); the planned capitalisation with those CDAs; a property sale to "PEARL S.R.L." with instalments allegedly met by the group (rec:flow:0076, 0077); payments to ITTI over voting machines (rec:flow:0078); a lottery-related company (src:acta-p23). The last three fall outside the charged facts.

**Corroboration needed.** Most of it is second-hand (what a colleague told him). The cash date sits oddly in his own chronology (rec:issue:0009). A co-accused's statement needs independent support: the email and attachment metadata, treasury records, CCTV and access logs, the receipt "payers", the CDA register, and testimony from the persons he names.

**Cooperation routes.** The framework found no general "colaborador eficaz" law. What exists: mitigation or waiver under Art. 196 inc. 9 and Art. 239 inc. 4 (only if those offences are in play); the criterio de oportunidad (CPP Art. 19); the abbreviated procedure (CPP Arts. 420-421), whose five-year threshold wording is unresolved in the framework; and ordinary sentencing mitigation. The defence offered "unrestricted" cooperation on the record (src:acta-p24). Any benefit would be tied to these narrow routes, not to a negotiated immunity.

## 9. Questions the record raises about supervisors

These are questions, not allegations. The supervisors also produced the analysis on which the case rests.

1. What exactly did the BCP approve in the 2024 feasibility plan, and did it cover lending to group companies on these terms? (Rey: rec:event:0044.)
2. If the regulator had "free, direct and permanent" access to the bank's movements, as Rey says (src:acta-p20), what was seen, and when?
3. The record says the October 2025 de-listing request received no formal reply (rec:event:0055); a press chronology says the regulator refused it that month (press:event:0004). Which is right, and why did lending to those companies continue to May 2026 without a recorded measure?
4. Press reports place internal supervision reports recommending rejection of the Vinanzas sale and a regularisation plan in July 2026 (press:event:0008, 0024), a sumario in September (press:event:0011) and resolution in November (rec:event:0067). Were the triggers in Ley 2334/03 Arts. 6-7 and the 24-hour rule in Art. 14 engaged earlier?
5. The criminal referral is reported for 3 Nov 2026 (press:event:0014). The framework found no express duty in the banking laws to report to prosecutors (CPP Art. 286 was not read). Was there an earlier duty?
6. How do the personal-liability rule for BCP directors (Ley 489/95 Art. 16) and the protection for officials in Ley 2334/03 Art. 27 apply? Press reports a separate case (Causa 92/2026) touching officials; it is outside this record.

## 10. What I could not rely on in the legal framework

- Exact statutory wording (all texts read through a paraphrasing tool).
- Art. 181 inc. 3 cross-reference (conflict unresolved).
- Art. 192 inc. 1 wording after 2008 ("obligación" or "responsabilidad").
- CPP Art. 420 threshold for the abbreviated procedure.
- Every BCP resolution, including the related-party cap. The press cites a 20% cap (press:fact:0083); the statutory 20% rule was repealed in 2016 and the current BCP limit was not retrieved, so no percentage is used here.
- Código Civil rules on directors and síndicos (one commentator only; síndico articles not retrieved).
- Whether Arts. 187 and 239 were amended after 2008.
- Case law: only press notes and one doctrinal paper; no judgment read.
